# Knowledge Cards: Why Bread Goes Stale in the Fridge

## Card 1｜Staling is starch retrogradation

**Key point:** Staling is driven mainly by starch retrogradation, not by water loss.

**Explanation:** Heat gelatinizes starch granules, which absorb water and swell. As bread cools, amylopectin chains inside the granules re-crystallize and push water back out of the starch structure. The crumb firms up while total moisture barely changes, which is why stale bread feels dry without being dry.

**Self-check:** Why can bread feel dry even though its moisture content has hardly dropped?

> Answer: Water has been expelled from the starch structure by retrogradation, not lost from the loaf.

---

## Card 2｜Retrogradation peaks in fridge temperature range

**Key point:** Retrogradation runs fastest between about -5°C and 4°C, the range a domestic fridge holds.

**Explanation:** At room temperature the process is several times slower; below about -18°C it nearly stops. The refrigerator therefore sits exactly on the worst band for texture, which is why chilling bread stales it faster than leaving it out.

**Example:** The source's comparison: fridge range -5°C to 4°C versus freezer below -18°C.

## Card 3｜Slow freezing damages structure

**Key point:** Freezing preserves texture only when it is fast; slow freezing forms large ice crystals.

**Explanation:** Large crystals rupture the gluten network and cell walls, so the loaf collapses on thawing. Fast freezing keeps crystals small and leaves the structure intact.

**Self-check:** Why can two loaves frozen at the same temperature end up with different textures after thawing?

> Answer: Freezing speed differs, and slow freezing produces large ice crystals that rupture the gluten network and cell walls.

---

## Card 4｜Wrapping prevents sublimation loss

**Key point:** Bread must be wrapped when frozen, because the freezer is a very dry environment.

**Explanation:** Unprotected bread loses surface water through sublimation and develops freezer burn. The wrap addresses surface desiccation, a separate mechanism from retrogradation.

**Self-check:** What does wrapping actually prevent in a freezer?

> Answer: Surface water loss through sublimation, which causes freezer burn.

---

## Card 5｜Reheating reverses staling only temporarily

**Key point:** Above roughly 60°C the crumb softens again, but staling resumes faster once it cools.

**Explanation:** Retrograded amylopectin re-melts on reheating, which is why toast tastes fresher. The reversal is temporary: after cooling, retrogradation resumes and typically runs faster than before, so a reheated loaf stales sooner the second time.

**Example:** The source's observation that toast and reheated bread taste fresher.

---

## Card 6｜The apple trick buys hours, not days

**Key point:** Storing bread with a cut apple or potato slows surface drying but does not stop retrogradation.

**Explanation:** The fruit adds moisture to the surrounding air, which addresses drying only. Since retrogradation is the main cause of firming, the remedy defers the crumb change by hours rather than days.

**Self-check:** Why is the cut-apple method unable to keep bread fresh for days?

> Answer: It only raises ambient humidity and slows surface drying; it does not affect starch retrogradation.
